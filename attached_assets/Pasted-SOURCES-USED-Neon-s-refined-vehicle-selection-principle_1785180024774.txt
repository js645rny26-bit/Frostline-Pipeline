Frostline Vehicle Viability Doctrine Patch

Audit the current vehicle-selection logic against the doctrine below. Make only the minimum changes necessary to remove any static assumptions that one market type is inherently safer than another.

Core doctrine

No wagering vehicle is inherently safer, more stable, or preferable by market type alone.

A vehicle becomes viable only after it independently clears the standards and gates specific to that vehicle. Only vehicles that clear their own gates may enter comparative selection.

After viability is established, rank the surviving vehicles price-blind according to:

1. coverage of the most common game scripts
2. number and severity of failure modes
3. conversion burden
4. timing dependence
5. starter leash or workload dependence
6. plate-appearance dependence
7. bullpen and game-state dependence
8. stability of the underlying baseball truth

Select the vehicle that captures the greatest share of realistic common scripts. Reroute only when that vehicle is unavailable.

Price may affect execution size or whether Neon chooses to wager, but price must never change the truth ranking or preferred vehicle.

Prohibited assumptions

Remove or prevent logic equivalent to:

* derivatives are inherently safer than full-game markets
* team totals are inherently safer than game totals
* starter props are inherently safer than sides or totals
* main markets are inherently safer than derivatives
* full-game totals are automatically preferable because they remove team allocation risk
* one vehicle class should always be checked or preferred first

There must be no universal vehicle hierarchy.

Vehicle-specific viability standards

Full-game Over

Must show:

* sufficient aggregate run-band separation
* multiple credible scoring paths, or one highly durable path with continuation
* starter and bullpen states that allow scoring to persist
* limited dependence on one exact team supplying all runs
* acceptable survival across common allocation outcomes

A full-game Over may outrank a team total when either offense can carry the scoring or when the model is confident in total pressure but uncertain about run allocation.

Full-game Under

Must show:

* suppression from both starter windows
* bullpen bridge support from both teams
* limited late-inning chaos, walk, homer, and defensive risk
* survival against one starter underperforming modestly
* no dependence on a single dominant pitcher carrying the entire thesis

Team Total Over

Must show:

* clear offensive ownership by one team
* stable lineup access and conversion depth
* a vulnerable opposing starter and/or bullpen continuation path
* limited dependence on the opposing team contributing
* enough scoring routes to survive one failed component

A team total may outrank a full-game Over when the scoring pressure is genuinely one-sided and the opposing offense faces a credible suppression chain.

Team Total Under

Must show:

* suppression against that specific offense through both starter and bullpen phases
* no major lineup, platoon, park, or late-game matchup escape path
* limited risk from short starter leash exposing a weaker bullpen

First-Five Total or Team Total

Must show:

* the thesis is concentrated in the starter window
* bullpen involvement would dilute or add unnecessary variance
* the required early conversion burden is realistic
* starter workload and expected innings support the chosen window

Starter Outs Under

Must show:

* traffic or pitch-count stress
* credible early-hook probability
* leash sensitivity
* multiple non-run-dependent failure paths
* the offense can pressure the starter even if run conversion is imperfect

Starter Earned Runs Over

Must show:

* actual run-conversion quality, not traffic alone
* stable damage and sequencing paths
* sufficient starter exposure before removal
* inherited-runner and scoring-rule risks are acceptable

Starter Hits Allowed Over

Must show:

* contact access is the dominant edge
* the thesis does not primarily rely on walks, homers, or early removal
* enough expected batters faced exist
* batted-ball and lineup contact quality are stable

Moneyline or Run Line

Must show:

* scoring advantage translates into win or margin probability
* bullpen and late-game state support the side
* the thesis is not merely total pressure with uncertain run ownership
* required margin conversion is realistic

Required selection process

For every game:

1. Establish price-blind game truth and run bands.
2. Identify the most likely game scripts.
3. Generate all coherent major vehicles.
4. Test each vehicle against its own viability gates.
5. Eliminate vehicles that fail their specific requirements.
6. Compare only the surviving vehicles.
7. Select the vehicle with the broadest common-script capture and lowest relevant dependency burden.
8. Reroute only if the preferred surviving vehicle is unavailable.
9. Return CORE or NO_CORE only. Every NO_CORE must name its blocker.

SEA–TEX regression example

Do not hard-code this game. Use it only as a conceptual regression case.

Underlying truth:

* aggregate scoring pressure existed
* the workbook leaned Over
* uncertainty remained over which team would supply the scoring
* a full-game Over captured both Seattle-led and Texas-led scoring scripts
* a Seattle team total introduced an unnecessary team-allocation failure condition
* Texas supplied the early eruption instead

Expected principle:

When aggregate scoring truth is stronger than team-specific scoring ownership, the full-game total should outrank the team total, provided the full-game total clears its own projection, continuation, and survival gates.

Required regression tests

Add or confirm tests covering:

1. Either-side eruption
    * Both teams possess credible scoring paths.
    * Full-game Over clears.
    * One team total has narrower script capture.
    * Full-game Over ranks first.
2. One-sided offensive ownership
    * One offense owns the pressure.
    * Opposing offense faces a strong suppression chain.
    * Team Total Over ranks ahead of Full-Game Over.
3. Traffic without dependable conversion
    * Starter faces walks, pitch stress, and hook risk.
    * Outs Under ranks ahead of ER Over and Hits Allowed Over.
4. Contact-dominant starter fade
    * Stable contact access is the primary edge.
    * Hits Allowed Over may outrank Outs Under or ER Over.
5. Full-game Under burden
    * One suppression chain is weak.
    * Full-game Under fails even when one starter is elite.
6. Price blindness
    * Changing market prices does not change game truth or vehicle ranking.
7. Unavailable preferred vehicle
    * Preferred vehicle is absent.
    * Engine reroutes only to the next highest-ranked vehicle that independently passed its gates.
8. No viable vehicle
    * All vehicles fail their own gates.
    * Output remains NO_CORE with the controlling blocker.
    * No vehicle is promoted merely to force a selection.
