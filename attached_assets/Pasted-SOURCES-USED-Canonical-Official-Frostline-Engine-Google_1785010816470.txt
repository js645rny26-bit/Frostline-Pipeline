Blocking findings

1. The historical decision record is not authoritative

This is the most serious defect.

VEHICLE_LOG retains the first occurrence of each game rather than the final pre-lock or final operational decision.

On July 24:

* Final RUN_LOG: 7 CORE
* VEHICLE_LOG: only 2 CORE

The five late CORE additions are absent as CORE decisions in the historical vehicle record. Therefore, the log cannot reconstruct the card that was actually issued.

It is worse for four July 25 games:

* 20260725_SEA_TEX
* 20260725_OAK_MIN
* 20260725_CIN_STL
* 20260725_LAA_SFG

Those game IDs appear in VEHICLE_LOG with:

* Date = July 24
* Direction = NONE
* Decision = PENDING
* no market line

Those invalid earlier rows precede the legitimate July 25 rows. Any analysis that “keeps the first row per game_id” will select the corrupted PENDING row and discard the valid decision.

This directly invalidates the planned monotonicity join.

Required behavior

The canonical historical row must be explicitly defined as one of:

* final pre-lock decision, preferred
* first CORE decision, if studying promotion behavior
* final board decision before first pitch

It cannot be “first row ever written.”

Every historical row must also pass:

Date encoded in Game_ID = row Date

before entering replay, monotonicity, or postmortem analysis.

2. The board lock does not fail safely when the first run occurs after cutoff

The per-game cutoff calculation is correct. The state transition is not yet governance-safe.

For the five early games, the first successful lock publish occurred well after cutoff:

Game	Lock delay	Time remaining before first pitch
KCR–DET	285 minutes late	Game already finished
ARI–WSN	110 minutes late	10 minutes
LAA–SFG	110 minutes late	10 minutes
TOR–BOS	105 minutes late	15 minutes
SD–MIA	105 minutes late	15 minutes

ARI–WSN and TOR–BOS were stamped LOCKED_IN approximately ten to fifteen minutes before first pitch, even though their intended lock time was two hours earlier.

Their Pre_Lock_Decision is therefore not proven to be the decision that existed at the cutoff. It is simply the decision present on the first successful post-cutoff publish.

This defeats the principal purpose of the lock.

Required behavior

When no preserved pre-cutoff snapshot exists, the first run after cutoff must do one of two things:

1. Recover a valid decision timestamped at or before cutoff, or
2. Default to LOCKED_OUT and require the named baseball-reason exception.

It must not retroactively declare a current decision “pre-lock.”
